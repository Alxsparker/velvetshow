Understand

The words of stage lighting, explained simply. One short definition per word, no jargon.

The lights

What we control

It all starts with a light that obeys orders.

Fixture

also “light”, “lamp”

A stage light you control remotely: you can change its brightness, its colour, and sometimes make it move.

Examples: an LED bar, a moving head (a light that moves), a beam.

Channel

one “slider” of the fixture

Each setting of a fixture is a channel: one for brightness, one for colour, one for movement… A channel takes a value from 0 to 255.

DMX

the language of lights

DMX is the standard way to tell a fixture what to do. It is a long list of channel values, sent over and over, many times per second.

Universe

a pack of 512 channels

A DMX universe holds at most 512 channels. A small show uses one; a big show uses several.

The moods

What we save

Nobody sets every slider by hand during the show. You save moods, then fire them.

Cue

say “kyoo”

A saved lighting mood: colours, movement, speed. It has a number and a name, like a photo filed in an album.

When you “fire cue 3”, all the lights take on that mood at once.

Drop

when it kicks back in

In a track, the moment the music bursts back in after a quiet part. That is where the lights should step up.

Backing track

accompaniment track

A recording (drums, bass, backing vocals…) the band plays back while they sing and play on top of it.

Click track

the metronome in your ears

A steady beat only the band hears, in their earpieces, so they play in time with the tracks.

Timeline

the line of time

A strip that represents the track from start to end. You place events on it: “at this moment, fire cue 2”.

The connections

What talks to what

There are two kinds of links: sound (an audio cable) and orders (the network).

Box

MaestroDMX

A small device that creates and plays the cues itself, then drives the fixtures. It costs about €800 to €900.

The Velvet apps do not replace the box: they tell it which cue to fire.

Mixer

the sound desk

The device that gathers all the sounds (mics, instruments, music) and sends them to the speakers. Allen & Heath CQ mixers are one example.

Line input

the box's ear

An audio socket on the box. It is the only way the box hears the music, and it must come from the mixer.

Mixer off or silent: the beams stay frozen, even if the cues are right.

Network and router

the “same network”

The router is the device that links your devices together (like the box in your home). Devices connected to it are on the same network and can talk to each other.

The Mac and the box must be on the same network.

Art-Net

DMX over the network

It is DMX travelling over the network, instead of a special cable. The box uses it to send the light to the fixtures and to the Mac.

OSC

a small message over the network

One app sends it to another to say “do this”. Here: “fire cue number 4”. Velvet Light DJ and Velvet Show send it to the box.

The message is called /show/cue/index and the port can be changed.

MIDI

another command language

Born in music, it also triggers things. Velvet Show can use it (or OSC) to fire cues.

In one line

Velvet Light DJ→ OSCMaestroDMX→ Art-NetFixtures

An app says which cue to fire (OSC). The box plays the cue and sends the light (Art-Net). The fixtures obey.

In the apps

The words you will see on screen

3D preview

Velvet Visualizer

Your lights, drawn in 3D on screen. It works with no gear at all: made for trying things before installing.

Live DMX

a Visualizer switch

To see the box's real light in the 3D preview, turn on the Live DMX switch. The light turns green.

Auto lights

the Velvet Light DJ switch

While it is paused, the app sends nothing. It starts paused.

Velvet Remote

the remote

An iPhone and iPad app that works as a remote for Velvet Show.
